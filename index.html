Eight things a microbiology deck generator should actually do

Not a generic AI slide tool wearing a lab coat. Every feature below exists because a real lecture on Klebsiella pneumoniae or C. difficile pathogenesis needs it.

01

Excellent PubMed + citation engine

Every generated deck is grounded in real PubMed abstracts pulled live via NCBI E-utilities — not hallucinated references. Citations attach per-slide and compile into a proper references slide.

NCBI E-utilities · keyless
02

Paper → structured slides

Give it a topic or paste findings from a paper, and it restructures them into a proper lecture arc: taxonomy, morphology, virulence, pathogenesis, diagnosis, treatment.

Dedicated micro/ID workflow
03

Evidence-focused, life-sciences first

This isn't a general-purpose deck maker with a science skin. Layouts, prompts, and sourcing are built around how microbiology is actually taught.

Domain-specific by design
04

Polished, brand-consistent layouts

Eight distinct layouts — title-and-image, two-column, full-bleed micrograph, clinical pearl, comparison table, section dividers, and a dedicated references layout — all sharing one coherent visual system.

Real diagrams, micrographs & GIFs

Wikimedia Commons, Openverse, and CDC PHIL supply free, properly-licensed images — including animated GIFs of processes like binary fission and biofilm formation — each with visible attribution.
